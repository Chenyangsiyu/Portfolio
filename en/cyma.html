Cyma

An app for discovering local, independent artists, turning the city itself into a playlist.

Role

Type

Year

Deliverables

01 — The problem

Music has never been more accessible. And yet we discover less and less.

Streaming platforms overflow with tracks, but algorithms decide what we listen to. We end up going round in circles with the same artists, and the independent scene stays invisible, even in our own city.

+100 000

new tracks uploaded every day

Observation

An abundant catalogue no longer guarantees diverse discoveries.

Observation

Recommendation systems shape how diverse the content we’re offered is.

Tension

Despite an ever-richer music offer, listeners miss out on a large part of the independent scene.

Design question

How might we bring music discovery back into our listening habits?

02 — User research

Understanding how people discover new artists, or don’t.

I ran an online listener survey: eleven questions in three parts, which collected 30 responses.

03 — Competitive analysis

Sofar Sounds

The competitor closest to Cyma’s idea: intimate gigs that put emerging artists in the spotlight.

Product & service

  • Intimate gigs in unusual venues
  • Spotlight on emerging artists
  • Immersive music experience

Marketing strategy

  • Communication built on experience and discovery
  • Surprise effect: line-up often kept secret
  • Building an engaged community

Strengths

  • Strong, distinctive experience
  • Showcases emerging artists
  • Local, human dimension

Weaknesses

  • Limited access, one-off events
  • No complete digital platform
  • Hard to scale

What Cyma takes from it

  • Keep what makes Sofar strong: live music, the human touch, local roots.
  • Add what it lacks: a digital platform to discover all year round, not just on the night of a gig.
  • Rely on a network of partner bars to offer regular gigs.

04 — The solution

Cyma connects listeners, independent artists and nearby venues.

A local music discovery platform with two rules: local artists only, and fewer than 10,000 streams.

06 — Logo

The wordmark is built from three parts. It moves freely between the six brand colours, and the standalone m monogram covers the app icon and small sizes.

07 — Type & colour

Two typefaces, two jobs: Unbounded, wide and punchy, carries the energy of the stage in headlines and taglines; Roboto Mono handles dates, venues and practical info, precise and legible like a setlist.

08 — Graphic system

A family of symbols born from overlapping arcs. They work alone as patterns or become frames that crop gig photos. The guidelines define two modes, free placement and repeat grid, plus do’s and don’ts for the logo.

09 — Applications

The system rolls out across the July season in Paris: campaign, programme, artist pages, street posters, tickets, wristbands and app icon.
